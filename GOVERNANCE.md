# Contribution and decision process

**Status: initial process for community formation and working-draft development.** This is not a claim of formal standards-body governance or ratification. The community can propose changes to this process through a public issue and pull request.

## Roles and authority

- **Participants** propose, discuss, and review changes publicly. Organization membership is not required.
- **Editors and reviewers** develop text and assess proposals. Named appointments and scope should be recorded publicly as the group forms.
- **Repository maintainers** triage issues and merge changes into working drafts. Initially, administration is held by the `EnterpriseReasoning-Org` GitHub account. Administrative ownership does not itself establish consensus or authority to ratify a standard.

The list of appointed editors, a multi-party steering structure, voting eligibility, quorum, and final adoption authority are still to be defined. White-paper authorship or organizational membership does not automatically appoint someone to these roles.

## From proposal to decision

1. **Open:** A participant opens an issue describing the problem, proposed change, alternatives, and acceptance evidence.
2. **Discuss:** Participants refine scope, raise objections, and identify affected use cases. Record technical decisions made in meetings back in the issue so people who were absent can review them.
3. **Draft:** An editor or contributor opens a linked pull request with concrete text and examples. The proposal remains a draft during review.
4. **Review:** For substantive technical or governance changes, announce a public review window of at least seven calendar days in the issue or pull request. Seek review from someone other than the author. If no independent reviewer is available, keep the proposal pending. Material changes restart the window for the changed substance.
5. **Resolve:** The maintainer posts a disposition and rationale, links review evidence, and records how objections were handled. Use one of: accepted into working draft, needs revision, deferred, or declined.
6. **Record:** Merge accepted working-draft changes with links back to the decision. Preserve the issue, discussion, and revision history. Acceptance into a working draft is not adoption as a standard.

Pure spelling, formatting, and broken-link corrections may use an expedited maintainer review. Changes in technical meaning, contribution rights, or decision authority are substantive.

## Consensus and objections

Seek reasoned agreement, not a count of reactions. Silence, a closed issue, or one organization's approval is not proof of community consensus. A substantiated unresolved technical objection keeps a proposal pending for revision or further evidence; maintainers should not describe that proposal as consensus-approved.

Participants should explain objections and the conditions that could resolve them. Maintainers may split a proposal, request experiments, or defer it with reasons. Record dissent even when a narrower part of a proposal proceeds.

## Reconsideration

Anyone may request reconsideration by opening an issue that links the earlier decision and explains new evidence or a process concern. Seek an uninvolved reviewer where available. If the founding group cannot resolve a contested decision, document the disagreement and defer formal adoption rather than imply that an independent appeals body exists.

## Releases and adoption

Publish early specifications as dated, versioned **working drafts** with change logs and open issues. A future candidate should include testable requirements, examples, conformance fixtures, interoperability evidence, and responses to review comments.

Formal adoption requires a separately published and reviewed charter defining who decides, how consensus or voting works, and how objections and appeals are handled. Until then, no release should be labeled an adopted standard or certified implementation.

## Founding decisions still open

Track these publicly before formal adoption: charter and scope; editor and maintainer appointment/succession; multi-party representation; specification and code licensing; contributor terms and patent policy; conduct/reporting process; adoption and appeal rules.

This document establishes an initial review workflow, not a patent commitment or legal agreement.
